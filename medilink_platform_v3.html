Severity: SEVERE — Concurrent Use Contraindicated
Warfarin (Anticoagulant / VKA) + Ibuprofen (NSAID)
Concurrent administration significantly potentiates anticoagulant activity. Ibuprofen inhibits platelet aggregation and competitively displaces warfarin from plasma protein binding sites, elevating free-drug concentration and substantially increasing haemorrhagic risk — including gastrointestinal haemorrhage and intracranial bleeding. Clinical recommendation: Substitute ibuprofen with paracetamol (acetaminophen) for analgesia. If NSAID therapy is clinically unavoidable, obtain haematology consultation, intensify INR monitoring (twice weekly), and counsel the patient on bleeding warning signs.

What is the maximum safe dose of Metformin for a patient with CKD Stage 2?
For CKD Stage 2 (eGFR 60–89 mL/min/1.73m²), Metformin is generally considered safe with appropriate monitoring. The recommended maximum daily dose is 2,000–2,550 mg administered in divided doses with meals. Key clinical considerations: (1) Monitor eGFR every 3–6 months; (2) Discontinue if eGFR falls below 30 mL/min/1.73m²; (3) Temporarily withhold 48 hrs before and after procedures involving iodinated contrast media. Current NICE (NG28) and ADA Standards of Care support continued use at this eGFR threshold. Recommend reviewing the patient's most recent renal function panel (serum creatinine, eGFR, urine ACR) before initiating or continuing therapy.

I missed my morning dose of Metformin today. What should I do?
If you have missed your morning dose of Metformin, take it as soon as you remember — unless it is close to the time of your next scheduled dose. In that case, skip the missed dose entirely and resume your regular dosing schedule at the next meal. Never administer a double dose to compensate for a missed one, as this may increase the risk of gastrointestinal side effects. If you frequently miss doses, consider setting a daily alarm reminder.
